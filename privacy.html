Privacy Statement
Hawaii Island Business Crime Watch (HIBCW)  ·  Last updated: November 6, 2026

1. Introduction

Hawaii Island Business Crime Watch (HIBCW) is a community crime-awareness program of the Kanoelehua Industrial Area Association (“KIAA,” “we,” “us”), kiaahilo.org. This Privacy Statement explains what information HIBCW collects, how we use and share it, and the choices you have.

It covers the HIBCW website at hibcw.kiaahilo.org, including the incident map and report form, the Join HIBCW membership application, business registration, and district alert emails.

2. Information We Collect

2a. When you submit an incident report

  • Incident type, date, time, and location (street address and/or map pin), and the district it falls in
  • A written description of what happened
  • Your name (optional) and email address (optional)
  • A police report number, if you have one
  • Suspect and vehicle descriptions, if you provide them
  • Photos or videos you choose to attach

2b. When you apply to join HIBCW

  • Full name, email address, and phone number
  • Business name, business address, and district
  • Any notes you add

2c. When you register a business

  • Business name, type, number of employees, and website
  • Physical and mailing addresses, and district
  • Contact name, phone number, and email address
  • Any notes you add

2d. KIAA staff and District Captains

For people who sign in to manage HIBCW, we keep their email address, name, role, district, and sign-in times.

2e. Collected automatically

Our hosting and database providers record basic technical information (such as internet address, browser type, and time of visit) to run the site and keep it secure. To prevent spam and abuse, HIBCW keeps a one-way scrambled (“hashed”) version of your internet address, together with the time of each submission, for up to 2 days. It is not stored with your report and is deleted automatically.

We do not use analytics, advertising, or tracking tools.

3. How We Use Information

  • Show community incident reports on the public map and incident list
  • Let KIAA staff and District Captains review, verify, and moderate reports
  • Show a “Verified KIAA Member” badge on reports from members
  • Review membership applications and business registrations, and connect businesses with their District Captain
  • Send district alert emails to approved members in the affected district
  • Follow up with you about a report, if you gave us your email
  • Share reports with law enforcement (see Section 5)
  • Spot patterns of criminal activity to support community safety
  • Keep the site secure and prevent spam and abuse

4. What the Public Can See

Anyone can view active incident reports. A public report shows the incident type, date and time, location and district, description, any police report number, any suspect and vehicle descriptions, any attached photos or videos, whether the problem is recurring, and whether the reporter is a verified KIAA member.

Your name and email address are never shown to the public, and the site does not send them to public visitors. Only KIAA staff and District Captains can see them.

Photos and videos are public. Each attached file is stored at a web address that anyone with the link can open. Before uploading common photo types (JPEG, PNG, WebP), the site removes hidden information such as GPS location and camera details. Videos and some other formats (for example, HEIC photos) may still contain this information, so consider turning off location tagging on your camera.

The District Captains list shows each captain’s name and district, and their email address and photo if they provided them.

Do not include sensitive personal information, such as Social Security numbers, financial account numbers, or medical information, in any part of a report.

5. Sharing of Information

We do not sell or rent personal information. We share it only as described here.

  • Law enforcement: reports and attached media may be shared with the Hawaiʻi Police Department or other agencies on request, or when we believe it will help with a crime or safety concern.
  • KIAA staff and District Captains: to review reports, applications, and registrations, and to run the program.
  • Service providers that run parts of HIBCW for us: Supabase (database, file storage, and staff sign-in), Vercel (website hosting), and Resend (email delivery). The map and address search use OpenStreetMap, its Nominatim address search, Esri satellite imagery, and County of Hawaiʻi district boundaries. These map services receive the addresses you search for and the technical information needed to deliver map images. Software libraries are delivered by unpkg and jsDelivr.
  • When required by law: for example, in response to a court order or subpoena, or to protect someone’s safety.

6. How Long We Keep Information

  • Incident reports are kept as a historical record of community crime activity unless they are removed. KIAA may remove or archive reports that are false, misleading, or submitted in bad faith.
  • Membership applications and business registrations are kept while the membership or registration is active, and as needed for KIAA’s records.
  • Anti-abuse records (hashed internet addresses) are deleted after 2 days.

7. Cookies and On-Device Storage

HIBCW does not use advertising or analytics cookies, and does not track you across other websites.

The site stores a few settings in your own browser so it works the way you left it, such as light or dark mode and whether you closed a notice. Staff sign-in also uses browser storage to keep staff signed in. These are needed for the site to work, are not used to track you, and you can clear them at any time in your browser settings.

8. Children’s Privacy

HIBCW is not directed to children. You must be 18 or older to submit a report, apply for membership, or register a business. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13; if you believe a child has submitted information, contact us and we will delete it.

9. Security

We use reasonable safeguards, including encrypted connections (HTTPS), access controls so only authorized staff can see reporters’ names and contact details, limits on repeated submissions, and removal of hidden location data from common photo types. No system is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee the security of information sent over the internet.

If a security breach affects your personal information, we will notify you as required by Hawaiʻi law (Hawaiʻi Revised Statutes chapter 487N).

10. Your Choices

  • You can submit a report without giving your name or email.
  • You can ask to see, correct, or delete the name, email, or other personal information you gave us, including membership applications and business registrations.
  • You can ask us to remove a report you submitted.
  • Approved members can ask to stop receiving district alert emails.

To make a request, email hibcw@kiaahilo.org. Reports that have already been shared with law enforcement may not be removable from their records.
